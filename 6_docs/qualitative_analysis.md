# Qualitative Analysis of Open-Ended Responses

## Family Planning Study

---

## Overview

Four open-ended questions were included in the questionnaire. These were excluded from the quantitative SPSS analysis
and are examined thematically here. Their findings will be incorporated into the results chapter alongside the
quantitative output.

| Variable | Total Responses |
|---|---|
| Why did you choose this method? | 172 |
| Husband's main concerns (if opposed) | 75 |
| Side effects experienced | 93 |
| Concerns about family planning methods | 384 |

---

## 1. Reasons for Choosing the Current FP Method

*Asked only of current users (n=174); 172 provided a response.*

### Thematic Categories 1

| Theme | Representative Responses | Approximate n |
|---|---|---|
| **Convenience / Availability** | "Available", "Available and cheap", "Available and temporarily" | ~43 (25%) |
| **Efficacy / Long-term suitability** | "Effective for long time", "Suitable for long time" | ~42 (24%) |
| **Health worker or medical advice** | "Health worker advice", "Medical advice", "Health care advice" | ~16 (9%) |
| **Husband's choice or influence** | "Husband choice" | ~17 (10%) |
| **Safety concerns** | "Safe", "Safe and effective" | ~17 (10%) |
| **Temporary use intention** | "Temporarily" | ~16 (9%) |
| **Other / Individual reasons** | Various | ~21 (12%) |

### Interpretation 1

The dominant driver of method choice was **practical accessibility** — women chose the method that was available and
affordable, rather than necessarily the method that best matched their medical or personal needs. A notable minority
(~10%) reported that it was their **husband's decision**, highlighting the significant role of male partner influence
in method selection, which is consistent with the quantitative findings on husband reaction. Health worker advice was
cited by approximately 9%, suggesting a role for provider influence but pointing to gaps in client-centered counseling.

## 2. Husband's Concerns When He Opposes Family Planning

***Asked only when husband opposes FP (n=76 oppose); 75 responses obtained.***

### Thematic Categories 2

| Theme | Representative Responses | Approximate n |
|---|---|---|
| **Religious / moral prohibition** | "Forbidden" | 23 (31%) |
| **Fear of infertility** | "Infertility", "Cause infertility", "Cause infertility and cancer" | 19 (25%) |
| **Side effects / health harm** | "Side effects", "Affect health", "Unsafe", "Cause disease" | 21 (28%) |
| **Desire for more children** | "Need more children" | 6 (8%) |
| **Fear of cancer** | "Cause cancer", "Cause breast cancer" | 3 (4%) |
| **Other** | Various | 3 (4%) |

### Interpretation 2

Husbands' opposition is primarily driven by three factors: **religious prohibition**, **fear of infertility**, and
**concerns about health/side effects**. The perception that contraceptives cause infertility is a significant
misconception that requires targeted health education targeting male partners. Religious framing ("forbidden")
reflects deeply embedded cultural attitudes in the community that are unlikely to be changed through information alone —
community-level and religious leader engagement are needed. Importantly, the quantitative data shows that 76 out of 335
women with known husband reactions (22.7%) faced spousal opposition, making this a major structural barrier to FP
uptake.

## 3. Side Effects Experienced

***Asked of those who ever experienced side effects (n=97 said yes); 93 provided descriptions.***

### Thematic Categories 3

| Theme | Representative Responses | Approximate n |
|---|---|---|
| **Menstrual disturbances** | "Intermittent vaginal bleeding", "Amenorrhoea followed injection", "Vaginal bleeding", "Recurrent vaginal bleeding" | ~36 (39%) |
| **Infections** | "Recurrent vaginal infections" | 7 (8%) |
| **Headache / neurological** | "Recurrent headache", "Headache and anxiety" | ~7 (8%) |
| **Weight gain** | "Increase weight" | 3 (3%) |
| **Mood / psychological** | "Low mood", "Anxiety" | ~4 (4%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | "Increase in blood pressure", "Cause thrombosis" | 2 (2%) |
| **Breast symptoms** | "Breast tenderness", "Breast pain and congestion" | 5 (5%) |
| **Method failure / unintended pregnancy** | "Unexpected pregnancy with injection", "Unexpected pregnancy with condom", "Unexpected pregnancy then abortion", "Abortion followed IUD" | 4 (4%) |

### Interpretation 3

**Menstrual irregularities are by far the most commonly reported side effect**, accounting for roughly 39% of all
descriptions. This is clinically consistent with the known side effect profiles of injectables and implants — the two
most common methods in this population. The mention of unexpected pregnancies (particularly with injectables and
condoms)points to possible **method failure or incorrect use**, which warrants counseling attention. The breadth of side
effects reported reinforces the need for pre-initiation counseling on expected side effects to prevent discontinuation.

## 4. Concerns About Using Family Planning Methods (All Respondents)

***All 384 respondents answered this question.***

### Thematic Categories

| Theme | Representative Responses | Approximate n |
|---|---|---|
| **No concerns** | "Non" | 217 (57%) |
| **Safety / general health harm** | "Unsafe", "Dangerous for health", "Affect the health" | 59 (15%) |
| **Religious / cultural prohibition** | "Forbidden" | 34 (9%) |
| **Fear of infertility** | "Infertility", "Cause infertility", "Permanent amenorrhoea" | 30 (8%) |
| **Fear of cancer** | "Cause cancer", "Cause breast cancer", "Cause cancers" | 12 (3%) |
| **Menstrual concerns** | "Intermittent vaginal bleeding", "Vaginal bleeding" | 7 (2%) |
| **Cardiovascular fear** | "Cause thrombosis" | 4 (1%) |
| **Social / relational fears** | "If husband knows about it" | 1 (<1%) |
| **Other / Side effects general** | "Side effects" | 4 (1%) |

### Interpretation

The majority of respondents (57%) reported **no concerns** about FP, which is an encouraging finding. However, among the
43% who expressed concerns, the dominant themes were **health and safety fears** (particularly infertility and cancer),
and **religious/cultural prohibition**. The fear of infertility is a well-documented barrier to contraceptive uptake in
Sub-Saharan African and Middle Eastern contexts and likely reflects both misinformation and the high social value placed
on fertility. Cancer fear may be linked to incomplete or incorrect information about hormonal methods. One respondent
specifically cited concern about her **husband finding out**, underscoring that for some women FP use is a covert or
unsafe behavior — an important protection and rights consideration.

| Open-ended Topic | Key Finding for Results |
